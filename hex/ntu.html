WHAT DOES ε-NTU CALCULATE?

From design check to predicted performance

Bell–Delaware calculates heat-transfer coefficients and pressure losses based on geometry and fluid properties. The effectiveness–NTU method (ε-NTU) uses the available conductance (UA), flow rates and inlet temperatures to predict achievable heat duty and both outlet temperatures, including at changed operating conditions.

Assumptions: steady state, single phase and constant properties within each calculation step. No phase-change or pressure-dependent flow model is included. Reference: MathWorks — E-NTU Heat Transfer.

FLOW ARRANGEMENT

ε-NTU configuration

The shell-and-tube equation is selected automatically from the shell and tube pass counts. Ideal counterflow and parallel flow are comparison models only.

FLUID PROPERTIES

Manual properties or fluid database

For each side, enter properties manually or obtain them from CoolProp at the estimated mean fluid temperature. In database mode, viscosity at the calculated wall temperature is also obtained. Properties are fixed within each manual calculation step.

Available fluids: water, 20 mass-% glycol/water mixtures, thermal oils, silicone oils and industrial heat-transfer fluids. Check the supported temperature range and the liquid phase before use. Out-of-range properties are rejected.

MANUAL FEEDBACK — NO AUTOMATIC ITERATION

One click = one new calculation

Bell–Delaware uses an assumed hot outlet temperature. ε-NTU predicts both outlet temperatures. The feedback button copies predicted hot outlet → assumed hot outlet and performs exactly one new calculation. Bell–Delaware recalculates the cold outlet from its energy balance. In fluid-database mode, properties are updated for the new estimate.
